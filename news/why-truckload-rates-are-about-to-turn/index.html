Spot Rates Are Up 39% — and It Still Isn't Enough

Rates rose across every equipment type for the first time since May. Record fuel is eating most of the gain.

Dispatch Lead, Brand Cargo
September 10, 2026 ← All news

Introduction

Two things happened in the same week. Total spot rates across all equipment types rose 9 cents to $3.22 per mile — up 39% against the same week last year, and the first increase across all three segments since May. And diesel set an all-time record at $5.967 a gallon. The headline number looks like a recovery. The margin underneath it tells a more complicated story.

1. Look At Rates Net Of Fuel, Not Gross

Before the March diesel surge, fuel-adjusted spot rates were running roughly 15-16% above the prior year. During the fuel spike they appeared to run 40-50% higher — but that was fuel recovery flowing through the rate, not stronger linehaul pricing. The most recent reading shows fuel-adjusted rates around 37% higher year over year, the softest comparison since April. Diesel is now consuming the improvement.

2. Know Which Number You Are Negotiating

If you quote all-in and fuel moves 37 cents in a week, you absorb it. If you quote linehaul plus a current surcharge, you don't. This distinction mattered less in a $3 diesel market. It is now the difference between a profitable lane and a break-even one.

3. Rates Are Rising Because Capacity Is Leaving

Fuel spikes have historically coincided with waves of carrier failures. Fleets without cost control or a working surcharge mechanism exit, capacity tightens, and rates firm for whoever remains. That is the mechanism behind the 39% — not a demand boom.

4. What This Means For Each Side

Shippers should expect firmer pricing through the fall and should treat quotes as short-lived. Carriers should be repricing forward, weekly, on live fuel — and should be honest about which of their lanes currently earn nothing after fuel.

Bottom Line

A 39% year-over-year rate increase alongside record fuel is not a boom. It is a market repricing risk in real time. The fleets that come out ahead are the ones measuring margin per mile, not revenue per mile.
