The Non-Domiciled CDL Rule Goes to Court Sept 15

The rule has been in force since March. A D.C. Circuit panel now decides whether it stays.

Compliance Manager, Brand Cargo
September 10, 2026 ← All news

Introduction

FMCSA's final rule on non-domiciled commercial driver's licenses took effect on March 16, 2026. On May 5 the D.C. Circuit refused to halt it, and oral argument on the merits is set for September 15. Whatever the panel decides, carriers are operating under the rule today. Here is the plain version — what it requires, who it removes from the workforce, and what to check in your files this week.

1. Eligibility Narrowed To Three Visa Categories

Only drivers holding H-2A, H-2B or E-2 nonimmigrant status can obtain, renew, transfer, upgrade or reinstate a non-domiciled CDL or learner's permit. An employment authorization document alone is no longer accepted. Applicants must present an unexpired passport and Form I-94 at every one of those transactions, and the license is capped at the length of the authorized stay or one year, whichever is shorter. Most work-authorized noncitizens — including EAD holders — are now ineligible.

2. Nobody Gets Pulled Off The Road Overnight

This is the most misunderstood part. A currently valid non-domiciled CDL stays valid until it expires. The driver is not stripped of anything at a roadside inspection. What changes is renewal: FMCSA's own analysis says roughly 97% of the approximately 200,000 non-domiciled CDL holders will not qualify when their credential comes up. That drains an estimated 30,000 to 40,000 drivers a year, one DMV transaction at a time.

3. Some Credentials Are Being Revoked

FMCSA has strongly encouraged states to audit all unexpired non-domiciled credentials and revoke any that were not compliant with the rules in effect when they were issued. Any noncompliant credential issued on or after March 16 must be revoked. If one of your drivers holds a license issued in a state now running that audit, treat the expiration date on the card as provisional.

4. Your Action List

Pull every driver qualification file and record license type, issuing state and expiration. Verify status before you spend money on testing a new hire. Build renewal tracking for non-domiciled credentials into your compliance calendar, because a surprise here costs you a truck, not just paperwork. And follow the September 15 argument — a merits panel is differently composed than the motions panel that denied the stay in May.

Bottom Line

The compliance risk sits with the carrier, not the driver. Clean qualification files protect you in a DOT audit regardless of how this litigation ends. For anything affecting a specific license, confirm with the issuing state agency.
